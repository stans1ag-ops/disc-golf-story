# The Little Red Hen
*A Classic Fairy Tale with 15 Storybook Illustrations & Voice Narration*  
*Source: https://americanliterature.com/childrens-stories/the-little-red-hen*  
*Adapted for Shane & Alex's Bedtime Stories*

Once upon a time, on a warm and sunny farmyard, lived a cheerful Little Red Hen. She had bright russet-red feathers, lively dark eyes, and a neat yellow beak. While the rest of the farm slept, she busily scratched and pecked in her picketty-pecketty way, always looking after her three fluffy yellow chicks.

*Illustration 1: `little_red_hen_01.jpg` — The Little Red Hen busily scratching in the sunny farmyard with her three fluffy yellow chicks.*

Sharing the farmyard were three very lazy friends: a plump pink Pig who loved snoring in the mud sty, a sleek calico Cat curled up asleep on the porch steps, and a chubby grey Rat snoozing by the warm hay bales. None of them ever wanted to lift a paw to help.

*Illustration 2: `little_red_hen_02.jpg` — The three lazy friends—the pink pig in the mud, the calico cat on the steps, and the grey rat by the hay—all fast asleep.*

One bright morning, while scratching near the wooden garden fence, the Little Red Hen uncovered something unusual in the warm soil. It was a slender, gleaming golden seed! "Cluck, cluck!" she gasped in wonder. It was a seed of wheat, full of promise and life.

*Illustration 3: `little_red_hen_03.jpg` — The Little Red Hen discovering the gleaming golden wheat seed in the rich dark soil.*

The Little Red Hen knew that if the seed was planted in the rich earth, it would grow into tall wheat to make flour for bread. She looked across the farmyard and called out cheerfully:

> "Who will help me plant this wheat seed?"

> "Not I!" grunted the Pig from his mud puddle.  
> "Not I!" yawned the Cat from the sunny porch.  
> "Not I!" squeaked the Rat from behind the hay.

*Illustration 4: `little_red_hen_04.jpg` — The Little Red Hen holding the wheat seed and calling out, while the pig, cat, and rat refuse to help.*

"Then I will plant it myself," said the Little Red Hen. And she did! With her industrious feet and bill, she carefully dug a neat little furrow in the garden soil, tucked the golden grain inside, smoothed the earth over it, and watered it with care under the morning sun.

*Illustration 5: `little_red_hen_05.jpg` — The Little Red Hen carefully planting the seed in the neat garden furrow and watering the soil.*

All through the long sunny summer, gentle rains fell and warm breezes blew. Day by day, a tiny green shoot poked through the dirt and grew taller and stronger, until a glorious stalk of golden wheat swayed proudly in the autumn breeze.

*Illustration 6: `little_red_hen_06.jpg` — The tall, golden stalk of ripe wheat swaying in the autumn breeze beside the garden fence.*

At last, the grain was ripe and ready. The Little Red Hen looked over at her resting companions and called out:

> "The wheat is ripe! Who will help me cut the wheat?"

> "Not I!" grunted the Pig, rolling onto his side.  
> "Not I!" meowed the Cat, lazily stretching her paws.  
> "Not I!" squeaked the Rat, twitching his sleepy whiskers.

*Illustration 7: `little_red_hen_07.jpg` — The Little Red Hen asking for help to cut the ripe wheat while the three lazy animals turn away.*

"Then I will cut it myself," said the Little Red Hen. And she did! She brought a little hand sickle from the tool shed and carefully reaped the tall golden stalk, laying the rich heads of grain gently upon the ground while her baby chicks chirped with excitement.

*Illustration 8: `little_red_hen_08.jpg` — The Little Red Hen using a sickle to harvest the golden wheat as her chicks chirp happily.*

Now the wheat stalks were gathered, but the golden kernels had to be threshed from the chaff. Hopeful and patient, the Little Red Hen called across the yard:

> "Who will help me thresh the wheat?"

> "Not I!" grunted the Pig with a heavy sigh.  
> "Not I!" purred the Cat without opening an eye.  
> "Not I!" squeaked the Rat, curling up tighter.

*Illustration 9: `little_red_hen_09.jpg` — The Little Red Hen asking who will help thresh the wheat as the lazy trio ignores her.*

"Then I will thresh it myself," said the Little Red Hen. And she did! After tucking her three downy chicks into their cozy nest for an afternoon nap, she patiently threshed the stalks until a neat wooden bowl overflowed with clean, plump golden grains.

*Illustration 10: `little_red_hen_10.jpg` — The Little Red Hen threshing the wheat, filling a rustic wooden bowl with clean golden grains.*

The plump grains were gathered, but they needed to be ground into soft white flour. The Little Red Hen poured the grain into a sturdy cloth sack and called out:

> "Who will carry this sack of wheat to the mill?"

> "Not I!" grunted the Pig, closing his eyes.  
> "Not I!" meowed the Cat, licking her front paw.  
> "Not I!" squeaked the Rat, tucking in his tail.

*Illustration 11: `little_red_hen_11.jpg` — The Little Red Hen standing beside the heavy sack of grain, asking for help to carry it to the mill.*

"Then I will carry it myself," said the Little Red Hen. And she did! Hoisting the sack over her wing, she trudged down the winding lane past clover fields to the old stone watermill. The friendly miller poured the wheat between the turning millstones, and soon she was carrying home a sack of fine, powdery white flour.

*Illustration 12: `little_red_hen_12.jpg` — The Little Red Hen visiting the rustic watermill where turning millstones grind the grain into white flour.*

Back home in the cozy farmhouse kitchen, the Little Red Hen tied a fresh white apron around her waist and put on a baker's cap. She set out the mixing bowl, yeast, and fresh water. Looking out the open kitchen window, she called:

> "Who will help me bake this flour into bread?"

> "Not I!" grunted the Pig from the shade of an apple tree.  
> "Not I!" chuckled the Cat from her rocking chair.  
> "Not I!" snickered the Rat from behind the flour barrel.

*Illustration 13: `little_red_hen_13.jpg` — The Little Red Hen in her apron and baker's cap by the kitchen window, calling out to her lazy companions.*

"Then I will bake it myself," said the Little Red Hen. And she did! She mixed the flour and water, kneaded the smooth dough with gentle care, shaped it into round loaves, and slipped them into the warm brick hearth oven. Before long, the most delicious, mouth-watering aroma of fresh baking bread drifted out the window and across the whole farmyard!

*Illustration 14: `little_red_hen_14.jpg` — The Little Red Hen sliding round dough loaves into the glowing brick hearth oven as wonderful steam rises.*

At last, the oven door opened, and out came two golden-brown loaves of bread, crusty and steaming and smelling wonderful. Instantly, the Pig, the Cat, and the Rat scrambled to the kitchen window, their eyes wide and their mouths watering hungrily.

The Little Red Hen looked at them through the window and asked:

> "Who will help me eat the bread?"

> "I will!" shouted the Pig, jumping to his feet.  
> "I will!" cried the Cat, leaping onto the sill.  
> "I will!" squeaked the Rat, hopping up and down.

"Oh no, you won't!" smiled the Little Red Hen with a cheerful cluck. "I planted the seed, I cut the wheat, I threshed the grain, I carried it to the mill, and I baked the bread. My three little chicks and I will eat it all ourselves!"

And she sliced the warm crusty loaf, spread it with sweet butter, and shared every delicious bite with her happy little chicks. And they did!

*Illustration 15: `little_red_hen_15.jpg` — The Little Red Hen and her three chicks happily feasting on warm slices of buttered bread inside the cozy kitchen, while the three lazy friends watch through the window.*
