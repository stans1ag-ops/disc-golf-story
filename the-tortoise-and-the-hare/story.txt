The Tortoise and the Hare.

A Hare was making fun of the Tortoise one day for being so slow. "Do you ever get anywhere?" he asked with a mocking laugh.

"Yes," replied the Tortoise, "and I get there sooner than you think. I'll run you a race and prove it."

The Hare was much amused at the idea of running a race with the Tortoise, but for the fun of the thing he agreed. So the Fox, who had consented to act as judge, marked the distance and started the runners off.

The Fox waved his flag, and the race began! The Hare shot forward like a brown lightning bolt, while the Tortoise set one steady foot in front of the other.

In no time at all, the Hare was so far ahead that the Tortoise was only a speck on the path behind him. The Hare laughed to himself. This race was already won, he thought.

The Hare was soon far out of sight, and to make the Tortoise feel very deeply how ridiculous it was for him to try a race with a Hare, he lay down beside the course to take a nap until the Tortoise should catch up.

The Hare curled up in the cool grass and was soon fast asleep, dreaming of his easy victory.

The Tortoise meanwhile kept going slowly but steadily, and, after a time, passed the place where the Hare was sleeping.

Step by step, never stopping, never hurrying, the Tortoise kept on toward the flower garland at the finish.

But the Hare slept on very peacefully; and when at last he did wake up, he opened his eyes wide in shock. The Tortoise was gone!

The Hare now ran his swiftest, stretching out in a desperate sprint with dust flying behind him.

There, just ahead, the flower garland fluttered over the finish line, and the steady Tortoise was almost there.

And then, to the great delight of the Fox, the Tortoise stepped across the finish line. He had won the race!

Moments later the Hare came panting up the path, too late. He could not overtake the Tortoise in time. He stared at the winner, and his long ears drooped.

Slow and steady wins the race. And from that day on, the Hare never mocked the Tortoise again, and the two became the best of friends.
