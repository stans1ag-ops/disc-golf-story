The Mystery of the Back Nine

A giant bronze feather, mysterious tracks by the creek, and a wild woodland surprise.

⏱️ 4 min read • 🎨 5 Illustrated Scenes • 🦃 Detective Adventure
Scene 1 • Hole 10 & The Back Nine

A cool afternoon breeze rustled through the tall oak trees as Alex and Shane walked past the little wooden sign that said Hole 10. They were officially on the back nine now, where the forest grew taller, darker, and much quieter.

"The back nine is where the real adventure begins, Shane," Alex said, swinging his disc golf bag over his shoulder.

Shane nodded with serious detective eyes. He reached into his small bag and held up his favorite disc: the bright orange Roadrunner. The shiny bird stamped on top caught the afternoon sun. Alex pulled out his own trusty flyer, bright bubblegum pink and ready for action.

"Hole ten is a long one, buddy," Alex pointed down the fairway. The green grass wound like a sleepy river between two rows of giant pines. "Show me a nice, flat rip."

Shane stood sideways on the tee pad, just like his dad taught him. He took one step, two steps, and whipped his arm.

Whoosh!

The orange Roadrunner zoomed down the middle of the fairway, spinning fast before gently settling near a big pile of dry leaves.

"Great rip, Shane!" Alex called out.

Scene 2 • The Giant Feather

Shane didn't run straight to his disc, though. He stopped suddenly on the dirt path. His eyes went wide as he pointed at the ground.

"Dad, look! A clue!"

Alex jogged over and knelt down beside his son. Right in the middle of the trail lay a feather. It wasn't an ordinary feather from a little robin or a blue jay. It was gigantic—almost as long as Shane's whole arm! It was patterned with stripes of chocolate brown and shiny black, and when the sunlight hit it, the edges glowed like warm bronze pennies. The very tip was dipped in creamy white.

Shane gently picked it up. "Who does this belong to, Dad? Is it an eagle? An owl?"

"It's definitely from a big bird," Alex said, turning the stiff feather over in his hands. "Look how strong the quill is. We are definitely sharing the woods with a mysterious neighbor. Keep your detective eyes open."

Shane tucked the feather into the side pocket of his disc bag like a flag. "Detective Shane is on the case!"

Alex stepped up to throw his pink disc. With a smooth flick of his wrist, the pink circle carved through the air, curving around an old birch tree and landing right next to the metal basket. They tapped in their putts—cha-ching!—and followed the winding trail toward hole eleven.

Scene 3 • Dinosaur Tracks by the Creek

The path grew narrower, dipping down near a bubbling woodland creek where the ground was wet and squishy.

"Wait," Shane whispered, putting up a little hand. "Dad, stop. Look down!"

Alex looked. Pressed deep into the dark, soft mud were several big footprints. They were shaped like giant arrows, with three long front toes and one little claw pointing backward.

"Whoa," Shane whispered, his eyes round as dinner plates. "Those look like tiny dinosaur tracks!"

Alex chuckled softly, kneeling down in the mud to get a closer look. "They sure do. Three long toes with sharp scratching claws. And look—there are big footprints, and right beside them are tiny, tiny baby footprints."

"The mysterious bird has a family," Shane declared. "And they're playing disc golf just like us!"

"They're definitely walking the same course," Alex smiled. "Let's see where hole twelve takes us."

Scene 4 • The Wild Turkey Family

Hole twelve tucked deep into an ancient grove of oak trees. The ground was blanketed with golden leaves and crunchy brown acorns. Shane lined up his throw with the orange Roadrunner. He swung his arm back and gave it a big heave. The orange disc sailed low and straight, skittering across the dry leaves near a hollow, mossy log.

Suddenly, a strange sound drifted through the trees.

Cluck... cluck... purrrrr.

Shane froze. Alex froze too.

Scratch, scratch, scratch.

It sounded like somebody was raking leaves with tiny forks. Shane crept forward on his tiptoes, making sure his sneakers didn't snap any dry twigs. Alex followed right behind him, placing a gentle hand on Shane’s shoulder.

Shane peeked over the top of the big mossy log. He gasped and grabbed Alex’s hand, pointing with excitement.

There, in a warm puddle of golden sunshine, was an entire wild turkey family.

The daddy turkey was huge. He was puffing his chest out like a big, proud balloon, with bright blue and red skin on his neck and a feathery beard hanging from his chest. His tail feathers were spread out in a giant, beautiful fan—and they matched the bronze feather Shane had found on hole ten!

Beside him was the mama turkey, quietly scratching the ground with her strong claws to flip over leaves. And running around her feet were four tiny, fuzzy baby turkeys, chirping in soft little chirps and gobbling up crushed acorns.

Shane’s orange Roadrunner was sitting right in the leaves, only a few feet away from them.

One of the fuzzy little babies waddled over to the orange disc. It tilted its head sideways, pecking gently at the edge with its tiny yellow beak: tock, tock.

Shane giggled silently. "Dad, he thinks my disc is a giant pumpkin," he whispered.

GOBBLE-GOBBLE-GOBBLE!

The daddy turkey let out a loud, proud sound: GOBBLE-GOBBLE-GOBBLE! The funny rumble echoed through the trees, making Shane cover his mouth to stop from laughing out loud.

The mama turkey called her babies with a soft purr-cluck, and the little chicks scampered into line behind her. Together, the whole turkey parade slowly waddled deeper into the forest, disappearing peacefully into the golden ferns.

Scene 5 • Case Closed!

Shane stood up tall and scooped his orange disc off the forest floor. He pulled the big feather out of his bag and held it up to the light.

"Mystery solved, Dad!" Shane cheered quietly. "The dinosaur tracks and the giant feather belonged to the wild turkeys!"

Alex tapped his pink disc against Shane’s orange Roadrunner with a cheerful *click*. "Great detective work, Shane. You tracked down a whole flock without scaring them away."

Shane smiled proudly, brushing a stray leaf off his disc. "Best back nine ever."
