# The Elves and the Shoemaker

*Adapted from "The Elves and the Shoemaker" (First Story) by the Brothers Grimm — public domain.*
*Source: https://americanliterature.com/author/the-brothers-grimm/short-story/the-elves-and-the-shoemaker*

*Adaptation note: this version tells the famous first story only. The elves wear simple ragged tunics (the original describes them as naked); the wife's gift of fine new clothes carries the same thank-you.*

## Scene 1 — The Last Piece of Leather

A shoemaker, through no fault of his own, had become so poor that he had nothing left but leather for one pair of shoes.

## Scene 2 — Cutting by Candlelight

That evening, he cut out the shoes he would make in the morning. Then, with a clear conscience, he went quietly to bed and fell asleep.

## Scene 3 — A Clear Conscience

He slept soundly all night long, never dreaming of the wonderful surprise the morning would bring.

## Scene 4 — Morning Wonder

In the morning, when he sat down to work, the two shoes stood quite finished on his table! He was astounded, and did not know what to say.

## Scene 5 — A Masterpiece

He picked up the shoes to look more closely. They were so neatly made that there was not one bad stitch in them, just as if they were meant to be a masterpiece.

## Scene 6 — The Generous Buyer

Soon a buyer came in, and the shoes pleased him so well that he paid more for them than was usual. With the money, the shoemaker was able to buy leather for two pairs of shoes.

## Scene 7 — Leather for Two Pairs

He cut them out that evening, and went to bed with fresh courage, eager for the morning.

## Scene 8 — Two Pairs by Morning

In the morning, the two pairs were already made! Buyers came gladly and paid him well, so he bought leather for four pairs of shoes.

## Scene 9 — Honest Independence

And so it went on. Whatever he cut out in the evening was finished by the morning, and soon he had his honest independence again, and in time became a wealthy man.

## Scene 10 — The Christmas Eve Plan

Now, one evening not long before Christmas, the shoemaker said to his wife, "What do you say if we stay up tonight, to see who it is that lends us this helping hand?"

## Scene 11 — Hiding and Watching

His wife liked the idea. She lit a candle, and they hid themselves behind the clothes hanging in the corner, and watched.

## Scene 12 — The Midnight Workers

At midnight, two tiny little men came in. They sat down at the shoemaker's table and began to stitch and sew and hammer so skillfully and so quickly with their little fingers that the shoemaker could not turn away his eyes for astonishment.

## Scene 13 — A Grateful Plan

The next morning his wife said, "The little men have made us rich, and we really must show that we are grateful. I will make them little shirts and coats and trousers, and knit them each a pair of stockings, and you shall make them two little pairs of shoes."

## Scene 14 — Gifts on the Table

When everything was ready, they laid the pretty little clothes on the table instead of the cut-out leather, and hid themselves to see what the little men would do.

## Scene 15 — Dance of Joy

At midnight the elves came bounding in. They found the little clothes and were overjoyed. They dressed themselves in the greatest delight, singing, "Now we are boys so fine to see, why should we longer cobblers be?" Then they danced and skipped right out of the door, and never came back again. But as long as the shoemaker lived, all went well with him.
