Rumpelstiltskin

By the Brothers Grimm • A bedtime tale about a miller’s daughter, straw spun into gold, and the power of a name.

Scene 1 • The Miller's Daughter

Once there was a miller who was poor, but who had a beautiful daughter.

Scene 2 • The Miller's Boast

Now it happened that he had to go and speak to the King, and in order to make himself appear important he said to him...

🧔 The Miller
“I have a daughter who can spin straw into gold.” — “That is an art which pleases me well,” said the King, “if your daughter is as clever as you say, bring her to-morrow to my palace, and I will try what she can do.”
Scene 3 • The Straw Chamber

And when the girl was brought to him he took her into a room which was quite full of straw, gave her a spinning-wheel and a reel, and said...

👑 The King
“Now set to work, and if by to-morrow morning early you have not spun this straw into gold during the night, you must die.”
Scene 4 • Tears in the Night

So there sat the poor miller's daughter, and for her life could not tell what to do; she had no idea how straw could be spun into gold, and she grew more and more miserable, until at last she began to weep.

Scene 5 • The Little Man

But all at once the door opened, and in came a little man...

🧙 The Little Man
“Good evening, Mistress Miller; why are you crying so?” — “What will you give me,” said the manikin, “if I do it for you?” — “My necklace,” said the girl.
Scene 6 • Whirr, Whirr, Whirr

The little man took the necklace, seated himself in front of the wheel, and “whirr, whirr, whirr,” three turns, and the reel was full. And so it went on until the morning, when all the straw was spun, and all the reels were full of gold.

Scene 7 • The Greedy King

By day-break the King was already there, and when he saw the gold he was astonished and delighted, but his heart became only more greedy. He had the miller's daughter taken into another room full of straw, which was much larger, and commanded her to spin that also in one night if she valued her life.

Scene 8 • The Ring

The girl knew not how to help herself, and was crying, when the door again opened, and the little man appeared...

🧙 The Little Man
“What will you give me if I spin the straw into gold for you?” — “The ring on my finger,” answered the girl.
Scene 9 • A Terrible Promise

The King rejoiced beyond measure at the sight, but still he had not gold enough; and he had the miller's daughter taken into a still larger room full of straw, and said...

👑 The King
“You must spin this, too, in the course of this night; but if you succeed, you shall be my wife.”
Scene 10 • The Third Night

When the girl was alone the manikin came again for the third time, and said, “What will you give me if I spin the straw for you this time also?” “I have nothing left that I could give,” answered the girl. “Then promise me, if you should become Queen, your first child.” And, not knowing how else to help herself in this strait, she promised the manikin what he wanted, and for that he once more spun the straw into gold.

Scene 11 • The Queen

And when the King came in the morning, and found all as he had wished, he took her in marriage, and the pretty miller's daughter became a Queen.

Scene 12 • The Little Man Returns

A year after, she had a beautiful child, and she never gave a thought to the manikin. But suddenly he came into her room, and said, “Now give me what you promised.” The Queen was horrorstruck, and offered the manikin all the riches of the kingdom if he would leave her the child.

🧙 The Little Man
“No, something that is living is dearer to me than all the treasures in the world.”
Scene 13 • Three Days

Then the Queen began to weep and cry, so that the manikin pitied her...

🧙 The Little Man
“I will give you three days' time; if by that time you find out my name, then shall you keep your child.”
Scene 14 • The Name Guessing

So the Queen thought the whole night of all the names that she had ever heard, and she sent a messenger over the country to inquire, far and wide, for any other names that there might be. When the manikin came the next day, she began with Caspar, Melchior, Balthazar, and said all the names she knew, one after another; but to every one the little man said, “That is not my name.”

Scene 15 • Not My Name

On the second day she repeated to the manikin the most uncommon and curious names she could find. “Perhaps your name is Shortribs, or Sheepshanks, or Laceleg?” but he always answered, “That is not my name.”

Scene 16 • The Fire in the Forest

On the third day the messenger came back again, and said, “I have not been able to find a single new name, but as I came to a high mountain at the end of the forest, where the fox and the hare bid each other good night, there I saw a little house, and before the house a fire was burning, and round about the fire quite a ridiculous little man was jumping: he hopped upon one leg, and shouted...”

🔥 The Little Man's Song
“To-day I bake, to-morrow brew, the next I'll have the young Queen's child. Ha! glad am I that no one knew that Rumpelstiltskin I am styled.”
Scene 17 • Rumpelstiltskin!

You may think how glad the Queen was when she heard the name! And when soon afterwards the little man came in, and asked, “Now Mistress Queen, what is my name?” at first she said, “Is your name Conrad?” “No.” “Is your name Harry?” “No.”

👸 The Queen
“Perhaps your name is Rumpelstiltskin?” — “The devil has told you that! the devil has told you that!” cried the little man, and in his anger he plunged his right foot so deep into the earth that his whole leg went in.
Scene 18 • The Child Is Safe

And so the Queen kept her child, and they lived in peace and happiness ever after. The end.

💭 Bedtime Chat

What should the miller’s daughter have thought about before making her promise?

🤝 Think Before You Promise The miller’s daughter promised her first child without thinking it through. Never promise something you aren’t sure you can give — a promise is a serious thing.
🕵️ Cleverness Wins The Queen saved her child by being clever and sending messengers to learn the little man’s name. Using your wits is better than giving up.
